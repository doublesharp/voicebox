---
title: TTS output trimming
description: Preserve internal pauses while guarding against post-silence noise.
---

Chatterbox and Chatterbox Turbo use `trim_tts_output` after each generated text
chunk. An internal silence of one second or more is now a candidate boundary:
it no longer discards every word that follows the first long pause.

The trim keeps resumed speech, including brief final words and modulated,
breathy continuations. It removes a continuation only when its active frames
look like stationary broadband noise: nearly constant energy and a flat
spectrum. The normal minimum is 300 ms of active noise. A 60–250 ms noise burst
also requires the preceding active audio to exceed a deliberately generous
reading-duration budget for that chunk's text. Pauses do not consume this
budget, and the budget alone cannot remove a voiced continuation.

The duration budget counts Unicode letters, wide-script characters and digits
conservatively, excluding bracket tags. It is a heuristic rather than speech
alignment. These waveform checks cannot reliably recognize hallucinated words
or establish that the requested text was spoken. Ambiguous continuations are
kept, so some nonstationary artifacts may remain for listening review.

Leading/trailing edge trimming and the cosine fade remain. The trim retains up
to 200 ms of the original trailing silence. A final partial analysis frame is
included so it cannot silently discard a short ending. Kokoro continues using
`max_internal_silence_ms=None` for edge-only trimming.

`generate_chunked` supplies each actual chunk or retry subdivision through an
optional `text` keyword when the trim callback accepts it. Existing
`(audio, sample_rate)` callbacks still work. The separate runaway retry detector
and its engine-specific settings are unchanged.

Deterministic regression cases cover voiced pauses, quiet room noise, breathy
continuations, obvious hiss, short endings, retry fallback and legacy callbacks.
Comparison artifacts from existing speech recordings belong outside the source
tree. A controlled pause inserted between existing recordings verifies sample
preservation, not how a real Chatterbox model chooses to pause or terminate.
